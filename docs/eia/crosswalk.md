# Regulatory Crosswalk

A single UNESCO EIA can satisfy overlapping assessment obligations across multiple frameworks. This crosswalk maps each UNESCO principle to equivalent provisions in the major AI governance frameworks so that organisations can conduct one assessment rather than several.

Running a UNESCO EIA before procurement or deployment satisfies the procedural requirements of the EU AI Act FRIA for high-risk AI systems (Article 27) and the NIST AI RMF's Measure function.

---

=== "NIST AI RMF"

    The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, 2023) organises AI risk management into four functions: **Govern**, **Map**, **Measure**, and **Manage**. The UNESCO EIA's three-part structure maps across all four functions.

    | UNESCO Principle | NIST AI RMF Function | NIST AI RMF Element | Notes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1. Proportionality and Do No Harm | Map | MAP 1.1, MAP 5.1 | Context-setting and risk identification |
    | 2. Safety and Security | Measure, Manage | MEASURE 2.2, MANAGE 2.2 | Technical robustness and incident response |
    | 3. Fairness and Non-Discrimination | Measure | MEASURE 2.5, MEASURE 2.6 | Bias testing and demographic disaggregation |
    | 4. Sustainability | Measure | MEASURE 2.2 | Environmental risk measurement |
    | 5. Right to Privacy and Data Protection | Map, Measure | MAP 1.5, MEASURE 2.10 | Data risk identification and privacy metrics |
    | 6. Human Oversight and Determination | Govern, Manage | GOVERN 1.2, MANAGE 1.3 | Human review policies and override procedures |
    | 7. Transparency and Explainability | Govern | GOVERN 1.1, GOVERN 1.7 | Transparency policies and explainability documentation |
    | 8. Responsibility and Accountability | Govern | GOVERN 1.2, GOVERN 6.1 | Roles and responsibilities; accountability structures |
    | 9. Awareness and Literacy | Govern | GOVERN 4.1, GOVERN 4.2 | AI literacy and training programmes |
    | 10. Multi-stakeholder Governance | Govern | GOVERN 1.4, GOVERN 5.1 | Organisational governance and stakeholder engagement |

=== "EU AI Act"

    The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) introduces risk-based obligations for AI systems. For **high-risk AI systems** (Annex III), Article 27 requires a **Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA)**. The UNESCO EIA satisfies and extends the FRIA's procedural requirements.

    | UNESCO Principle | EU AI Act Article | Provision | Notes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1. Proportionality and Do No Harm | Art. 9 | Risk management system | Iterative risk identification and mitigation |
    | 2. Safety and Security | Arts. 9, 15 | Risk management; accuracy and robustness | Technical performance requirements |
    | 3. Fairness and Non-Discrimination | Arts. 10, 27 | Data governance; FRIA | Data bias requirements and fundamental rights assessment |
    | 4. Sustainability | Recital 48 | Environmental considerations | Obligation to consider sustainability |
    | 5. Right to Privacy and Data Protection | Arts. 10, 27 | Data governance; FRIA | Personal data handling; rights of data subjects |
    | 6. Human Oversight and Determination | Art. 14 | Human oversight | Technical and organisational human oversight measures |
    | 7. Transparency and Explainability | Arts. 13, 50 | Transparency; disclosure obligations | Technical documentation and user notification |
    | 8. Responsibility and Accountability | Arts. 16–29 | Provider/deployer obligations | Supply chain accountability and conformity assessment |
    | 9. Awareness and Literacy | Art. 4 | AI literacy | Obligation to ensure adequate AI literacy |
    | 10. Multi-stakeholder Governance | Arts. 27, 99 | FRIA; market surveillance | Stakeholder consultation and regulatory cooperation |

    !!! note "FRIA Alignment"
        The EU AI Act Article 27 FRIA requires deployers of high-risk AI systems to assess fundamental rights impacts before deployment. The UNESCO EIA Part 1 (Scoping) and Part 3 (Impact Mapping) directly fulfil the FRIA's procedural requirements. Completing a UNESCO EIA constitutes a documented FRIA for Article 27 purposes, subject to legal review in the applicable jurisdiction.

=== "OECD Principles"

    The OECD AI Principles (2019, updated 2024) are organised into five value-based principles for responsible stewardship and five principles for governments. The UNESCO EIA's 10 principles subsume and extend the five OECD value-based principles.

    | UNESCO Principle | OECD AI Principle | OECD Reference | Notes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1. Proportionality and Do No Harm | Inclusive growth and well-being | OECD P1.1 | Shared benefit and human flourishing |
    | 2. Safety and Security | Robustness, security and safety | OECD P1.4 | Technical safety throughout lifecycle |
    | 3. Fairness and Non-Discrimination | Inclusive growth; Human-centred values | OECD P1.1, P1.2 | Equity and non-discrimination |
    | 4. Sustainability | Inclusive growth and well-being | OECD P1.1 | Long-term environmental sustainability |
    | 5. Right to Privacy and Data Protection | Human-centred values and fairness | OECD P1.2 | Privacy and data protection |
    | 6. Human Oversight and Determination | Human-centred values and fairness | OECD P1.2 | Human control and autonomy |
    | 7. Transparency and Explainability | Transparency and explainability | OECD P1.3 | Direct mapping to OECD P1.3 |
    | 8. Responsibility and Accountability | Accountability | OECD P1.5 | Direct mapping to OECD P1.5 |
    | 9. Awareness and Literacy | Accountability | OECD P1.5 | Informed stakeholders enable accountability |
    | 10. Multi-stakeholder Governance | Multi-stakeholder governance | OECD P2.2 | International cooperation and adaptive governance |

=== "ISO/IEC 42001"

    ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the international standard for AI Management Systems (AIMS). It provides a PDCA-cycle framework for governing AI responsibly. The UNESCO EIA maps to both the normative clauses and Annex A controls.

    | UNESCO Principle | ISO/IEC 42001 Element | Reference | Notes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1. Proportionality and Do No Harm | AI impact assessment | Clause 6.1, A.8 | Risk and opportunity assessment; impact assessment |
    | 2. Safety and Security | AI system lifecycle | A.6 | Technical controls across development lifecycle |
    | 3. Fairness and Non-Discrimination | Data for AI | A.7.2 | Data quality, representativeness, and bias controls |
    | 4. Sustainability | Operational planning | Clause 8.4 | Resource and environmental considerations |
    | 5. Right to Privacy and Data Protection | Data governance | A.7, Clause 6.1.3 | Privacy controls and data protection requirements |
    | 6. Human Oversight and Determination | Human oversight | A.6.2.8 | Human review and control mechanisms |
    | 7. Transparency and Explainability | Transparency | A.6.2.4 | Transparency controls and documentation |
    | 8. Responsibility and Accountability | Leadership and accountability | Clause 5.3 | Roles, responsibilities, and authorities |
    | 9. Awareness and Literacy | Competence and awareness | Clauses 7.2, 7.3 | Training and awareness requirements |
    | 10. Multi-stakeholder Governance | Context of the organisation | Clause 4.2 | Understanding interested parties and governance context |

=== "CoE HUDERIA"

    The Council of Europe's Human Rights, Democracy and Rule of Law Impact Assessment (HUDERIA) is a methodology for assessing AI impacts on CoE core values. It is structured around five stages that parallel the UNESCO EIA's 6-stage process.

    | UNESCO Principle | HUDERIA Stage | Description | Notes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1. Proportionality and Do No Harm | Stage 1: Scoping | System definition and proportionality test | UNESCO Part 1 and HUDERIA Stage 1 are directly comparable |
    | 2. Safety and Security | Stage 3: Risk Assessment | Technical risk identification | Safety risks identified in systematic assessment |
    | 3. Fairness and Non-Discrimination | Stage 3: Risk Assessment | Non-discrimination risk matrix | Aligns with HUDERIA's democratic integrity concerns |
    | 4. Sustainability | Stage 2: Context Analysis | Broader societal context | Environmental sustainability as part of context |
    | 5. Right to Privacy and Data Protection | Stage 3: Risk Assessment | Privacy impact assessment | Data protection rights as human rights |
    | 6. Human Oversight and Determination | Stage 4: Mitigation | Human control safeguards | HUDERIA Stage 4 centres human override mechanisms |
    | 7. Transparency and Explainability | Stage 4: Mitigation | Transparency safeguards | Public disclosure and explainability commitments |
    | 8. Responsibility and Accountability | Stage 5: Monitoring | Ongoing accountability | Monitoring and accountability through lifecycle |
    | 9. Awareness and Literacy | Stage 2: Context Analysis | Societal context and capacity | Literacy as a precondition for meaningful rights exercise |
    | 10. Multi-stakeholder Governance | Stage 5: Monitoring | Multi-stakeholder monitoring | CoE HUDERIA emphasises public sector multi-stakeholder oversight |

## Cross-Framework Summary

Running a UNESCO EIA before procurement or deployment satisfies the procedural requirements of the EU AI Act FRIA for high-risk AI systems (Article 27) and the NIST AI RMF's Measure function. The table below summarises coverage:

| Framework | Coverage by UNESCO EIA | Gap (if any) |
|---|---|---|
| **EU AI Act FRIA** (Art. 27) | Full procedural coverage via Parts 1–3 | Jurisdiction-specific legal review required |
| **NIST AI RMF** | Map, Measure, Manage functions covered; Govern requires supplemental policy work | Internal governance policies needed |
| **OECD AI Principles** | Full value coverage across all 5 principles | OECD P2 (government obligations) requires public sector specific work |
| **ISO/IEC 42001** | Impact assessment and risk controls covered | AIMS certification requires full management system implementation |
| **CoE HUDERIA** | Stages 1–4 covered; Stage 5 (monitoring) requires ongoing programme | Post-deployment monitoring plan needed |
