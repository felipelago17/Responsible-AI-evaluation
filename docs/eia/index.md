# UNESCO Ethical Impact Assessment

The **Ethical Impact Assessment (EIA)** is a structured methodology developed by UNESCO to help organisations identify, evaluate, and mitigate the ethical risks posed by artificial intelligence systems. It operationalises the *UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence* (2021) — the first global normative instrument on AI ethics, adopted unanimously by 193 Member States at the 41st session of the UNESCO General Conference.

The EIA translates the Recommendation's 4 core values and 10 principles into a practical, multi-stakeholder assessment process that can be embedded in procurement, development, and deployment workflows.

## Who Is the EIA For?

The EIA is designed for any actor that develops, deploys, procures, or governs AI systems:

| Actor | Use Case |
|---|---|
| **AI developers** | Embed ethical safeguards early in the design lifecycle |
| **Deploying organisations** | Assess systems before go-live or major updates |
| **Procurement teams** | Evaluate vendor AI products against ethical criteria |
| **Governments and regulators** | Require or verify EIAs as part of conformity assessment |
| **Civil society and affected communities** | Participate in stakeholder engagement (Stage 5) |
| **Auditors and compliance teams** | Cross-reference with EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and other frameworks |

---

## When to Run an EIA

An EIA should be conducted:

- **Before initial deployment** of any AI system that affects individuals or groups
- **Before major updates** that change the system's scope, data sources, or decision logic
- **During procurement decisions** when selecting AI products or services from vendors
- **Periodically** as part of ongoing governance and monitoring (suggested annually or at major version changes)
- **When regulatory requirements change** in applicable jurisdictions

---

## Why Run an EIA?

- **Alignment with the UNESCO Recommendation (2021):** The EIA is the official tool for implementing the Recommendation's ethical principles in practice.
- **Regulatory readiness:** An EIA supports compliance with the EU AI Act Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (Article 27), NIST AI RMF Measure function, and ISO/IEC 42001 risk assessment requirements. See the [Regulatory Crosswalk](crosswalk.md).
- **Stakeholder trust:** Structured engagement with affected communities builds transparency and accountability.
- **Risk reduction:** Systematic impact mapping surfaces harms before they occur, enabling targeted mitigation.

---

## Structure: 3 Parts and 6 Stages

The EIA is organised into **3 parts** (what to assess) and **6 stages** (how to conduct the assessment):

### The 3 Parts

| Part | Title | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Part 1** | [Scoping Questions](scoping.md) | Define the AI system, its context, affected stakeholders, and regulatory environment |
| **Part 2** | [Principles & Procedural Safeguards](principles.md) | Evaluate the system against each of the 10 UNESCO ethical principles |
| **Part 3** | [Mapping Potential Impacts](impact-mapping.md) | Map positive and negative impacts by likelihood and severity; plan mitigations |

### The 6 Stages

| Stage | Name | Key Activity |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | Project Management | Assign lead, form team, set timeline and scope |
| **Stage 2** | Constituting the Diverse Team | Assemble multidisciplinary, cross-functional team |
| **Stage 3** | Asynchronous Work | Each member independently completes the assessment |
| **Stage 4** | Collaborative Workshop | Team convenes to reconcile findings and resolve disagreements |
| **Stage 5** | Stakeholder Engagement | Engage affected communities and incorporate feedback |
| **Stage 6** | End-of-Assessment | Sign-off, publish findings, version-control the assessment |

## The 4 Core Values

The UNESCO Recommendation grounds its principles in four overarching values:

1. **Respect, protection and promotion of human rights and fundamental freedoms and human dignity**
2. **Environment and ecosystem flourishing**
3. **Ensuring diversity and inclusiveness**
4. **Living in peaceful, just and interconnected societies**

See [Principles Reference](principles-reference.md) for the full value and principle descriptions.
